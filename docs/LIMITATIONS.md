# Limitations

BeaconScope is an offline prototype and analyst triage aid. Its features detect channel behavior, not malware identity or operator intent.

## Detection limits

- **Periodic benign activity:** NTP, updates, telemetry, backups, and monitoring can score highly. In the seeded synthetic evaluation, three periodic benign channels were false positives before fan-in suppression.
- **Suppression risk:** A benign baseline can become stale or contaminated. Shared C2 infrastructure may have high fan-in and be suppressed. Review the suppression reason and preserve raw scores.
- **Jitter:** A single seeded six-hour sweep detected its three beacon channels at each tested jitter fraction through 50%. This does not establish performance above 50%, across other seeds, or under real network delay and loss.
- **Timing gate:** Alerts require at least half the intervals within the 30% median band or a spectral score of at least 0.4. This gate reduced an alert on the existing irregular local capture, but is heuristic and may miss sparse or heavily jittered beacons.
- **TCP:** The timestamp is an outbound SYN. Captures missing the initiating SYN miss that event. Retransmissions are collapsed while a tuple is active; capture truncation and tuple reuse can still affect event identity. Connections that reuse one source/destination/port channel are aggregated by channel, as intended.
- **UDP:** Beacon events use the first observed datagram per directional five-tuple. Repeated messages on a persistent tuple are not counted separately; changing source ports create additional events. This is not equivalent to application transaction detection.
- **DNS:** The sidecar only analyzes decoded DNS query names visible in the capture. Domain grouping approximates the registrable domain using the final two labels and may be wrong for public suffixes. DNS-over-HTTPS and DNS-over-TLS are not decoded. DNS features have unit tests but no labeled DNS-tunneling evaluation and do not affect alert scores.
- **Encryption and attribution:** TLS payloads are not decrypted; BeaconScope does not associate flows with endpoint processes. IP/port and timing do not establish who generated traffic or whether it is malicious.
- **NAT and shared infrastructure:** NAT can combine hosts into one source address; shared proxies/CDNs can weaken destination context. A capture may not expose the true client or destination service.
- **Observation window:** Persistence, query rate, and autocorrelation depend on capture duration and start/end boundaries. Missing packets and clock/timestamp resolution affect calculated intervals.

## Performance limits

The parser uses Scapy's streaming reader and retains channel events rather than all packet objects. No 50 MB/200 MB profile was available, so throughput and peak memory are unmeasured. Very high channel/event cardinality and long captures can still consume significant memory, especially for per-channel event arrays and FFT analysis.

## Evaluation limits

The current metrics come from a small seeded synthetic dataset generated by this repository. The existing local PCAPs are smoke tests without channel-level labels. No public held-out malicious capture, production benign set, RITA comparison, or detection-latency measurement has been completed. Suppression's perfect synthetic result assumes predeclared topology that is intentionally easy to distinguish.

Do not describe the current results as real-world detection accuracy or claim validated precision/recall on malware traffic. Expand the labeled held-out set, document dataset terms, freeze the threshold before evaluation, and report confidence intervals and per-dataset sample counts before making performance claims.
